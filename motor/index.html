Meet the motor

Magnets make a field; a coil of wire carries current; the commutator and brushes feed it.

The battery pushes current in through one brush, around the coil, and out through the other. The coil and the commutator turn on the shaft; the magnets and brushes stay still.

1. A push on a wire

A wire carrying current across a magnetic field feels a force. Use your left hand: First finger along the Field, seCond finger along the Current, and your thuMb points to the Motion.

F = BIL. 20 turns × 0.5 T × 3 A × 0.05 m = 1.5 N on each side, in opposite directions, so the coil turns.

Arrows show conventional current (+ to −). The electrons in the wire actually flow the other way.

2. The commutator

Without a commutator the force flips past vertical and the coil just rocks. The split ring swaps the connections every half turn, so the current reverses and the coil keeps turning.

Keep scrolling: first with two plain slip rings, then with the split-ring commutator. The gauge shows the turning effect of one loop.

3. Speed and back-EMF

At the start the current is the full 3 A (6 V ÷ 2 Ω). As the coil speeds up it acts as a generator, making a back-EMF that cancels more of the battery voltage, until the speed settles near 2700 rpm.

Put a load on the shaft and it slows down, the back-EMF falls, and it draws more current to push harder.

One loop is drawn so it's clear; the numbers come from a motor with many coils, so its turning force is smooth. Coil inductance is ignored. Shown 20× slower.

4. Run it backwards: a generator

Spin it and it makes electricity: EMF = kω. At 1000 rpm that's 2.1 V. This is regenerative braking.

The battery is swapped for a bulb. The faster you turn the shaft, the brighter it glows — and the harder the coil pushes back against you.

5. Motors in electric cars

Electric-car motors have no brushes: electronics switch the coils. They are about 90 % efficient (a petrol engine is about 30 %) and give full torque from 0 rpm.

Many cars use AC induction motors, where the spinning part has no wires connected to it at all. Compare with how a petrol engine works.
